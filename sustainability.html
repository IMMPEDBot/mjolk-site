Lagom — the Swedish word for just enough.

Less, but better.

We're a small cookie business. We're not going to claim we're saving the planet. But here's exactly what we do, what we don't, and what we're working on. Honest version.

Most of what's wrong with food businesses isn't malice — it's defaults. Default packaging, default shipping, default suppliers. We've tried, where we can afford to, to pick the harder default. We're not perfect at it. Here's the audit.

i.

The tin is the point

Every tin is a powder-coated steel keepsake — not single-use packaging. We chose 18cm-diameter steel tins specifically because they last decades and have a dozen second-uses (we collect customer photos: tea bags, sewing kits, dog treats, Lego, in one case a proposal ring).

The tin is more carbon-intensive than a cardboard box upfront, but if it's used 50+ times the maths works out comfortably better. The hand-painted Dala horse on each lid is meant to make it feel like a thing worth keeping.

ii.

UK ingredients first

Where we can buy British without compromising taste, we do. Shipton Mill flour from the Cotswolds. Free-range eggs from a single farm in Pershore, Worcestershire. Cultured butter from a Lincolnshire creamery. Maldon sea salt from Essex.

What we import: chocolate (no British chocolate is in the league of Callebaut and Valrhona — sorry), pistachio cream (Sicily), and vanilla (Madagascar). We've looked. We won't compromise on these.

iii.

Packaging — what's in the tin and around it

Inside the tin: a sheet of FSC-certified greaseproof paper. That's it. No plastic, no foil, no liner.

Around the tin (UK-wide deliveries): a vacuum-sealed compostable cellulose inner bag (made by Tipa in Israel, certified to EN13432) and a recycled-cardboard outer box. The shipping label is recycled paper with non-toxic adhesive.

Around the tin (Birmingham same-day): nothing. The courier carries the tin in an insulated thermal bag that gets returned to the kitchen, washed, and reused. Zero shipping packaging on local deliveries.

iv.

Delivery emissions

Same-day Birmingham deliveries are made by cargo bike for postcodes within 5km of the kitchen (B1–B19, B30, parts of B5, B12, B13). That's roughly 60% of our same-day volume, with zero delivery emissions.

Beyond that radius we use Zedify electric vans (Birmingham's emission-free last-mile partner) for the remaining West Midlands postcodes. UK-wide overnight goes via DPD, which uses electric vehicles for last-mile in major UK cities and offsets the rest. We pay for the offset surcharge — we don't pass it on.

v.

Suppliers we'd like to be associated with

Every supplier we work with has been visited at least once, by someone from the bakery. Not a video call — a visit. We don't have written ethical sourcing policies because we're too small for that to mean anything; we just buy from people we've met.

If a supplier's working practices change, we'll find another supplier. We dropped a chocolate distributor in October 2025 over a labour-rights story we couldn't verify. We don't name them publicly because we couldn't verify it — but we moved.

vi.

What we haven't sorted yet

Energy: our Digbeth kitchen runs on the building's standard supply, which is Octopus's "Flexible" tariff — partly renewable, not fully. We can't unilaterally switch the building. We're pushing the landlord; he says he's looking. We'll keep pushing.

Vegan range: a properly good vegan cookie is on the 2026 roadmap. Until we have one we're proud of, we'd rather not bluff.

Tin take-back: we've been asked. We're working on a "send 5 tins back, get 1 free cookie" scheme for late 2026 — the logistics are fiddlier than they look (sterilising returned steel for food contact). Working on it.

A genuine note

We're a cookie business.

We aren't a sustainability brand. We don't think a £30 cookie tin is a meaningful contribution to the climate fight, and we'd rather not pretend that buying our cookies is a green act. We make a small treat that gets driven across a small city in modest volumes.

What we can do is take the basic decisions seriously — packaging, sourcing, delivery, energy. That's what this page is for. If you want to spot-check us on any of it, please do — email hello@mjolk.co.uk and we'll send you receipts.
