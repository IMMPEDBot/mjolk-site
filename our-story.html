A bakery, a Swedish word, and Birmingham.

How a Brummie bakery
fell for a Swedish word.

We started as a Tuesday-night side hustle in a Moseley kitchen in 2024. We're now a five-person bakery in Digbeth, sending warm cookie tins across the West Midlands every day. This is the short version of how that happened — and why everything we do is built around a Swedish word that doesn't quite translate.

The kitchen on Anderton Road.

Mjölk started where most small food businesses start — late, in someone's kitchen, after a long day at a different job. Our founder Lina Karlsson grew up in Stockholm, moved to Birmingham in 2018 for a design role at a Brindleyplace agency, and spent six years missing two specific things: the cookies her grandmother (mormor) made on Saturday mornings, and the workplace fika culture that the UK office she was now in had quietly never heard of.

The cookies came first. Lina had Mormor's recipe written on the back of an envelope — brown butter, dark muscovado, two days' rest, properly molten Belgian milk chocolate. She'd made them for friends in Birmingham for years, and at some point in early 2024 a friend asked if she could buy a batch for a colleague's leaving do. Lina baked twelve cookies on a Tuesday night, wrapped them in a £4 tin from John Lewis, and delivered them by bike across town.

The friend's whole office texted Lina the following week. The leaving-do guy still had the tin. That was the start of it.

The cookies are the product. Fika is the reason.

Why a Swedish word.

We knew the cookies were good — that bit was straightforward. The harder question, by autumn 2024, was what we wanted to be. Birmingham already has good bakeries. Adding another would have been fine, but not interesting. The thing that kept coming up in customer messages was not actually the cookies. It was that people were eating them together. Tins were arriving at offices and the whole team was stopping. Tins were arriving at homes and partners were sitting down. People were texting us photos of their kitchen tables.

That was fika. That was what was missing in most British workplaces and most British evenings — not better biscuits, but the permission to stop. We could keep making cookies and call it a cookie company, or we could make cookies as a vehicle for the actual thing, which was a daily pause. The second framing felt true. So we built the brand around it.

The move to Digbeth.

In March 2025 we outgrew the Moseley kitchen and took a unit at Floodgate Studios on Floodgate Street, in the bit of Digbeth that smells faintly of malt all week because of the brewery next door. It's a converted Victorian factory floor with white-tiled walls, three industrial ovens, a courier rack by the back door, and a hand-painted sign over the entrance that says "välkommen — kram, mormor" in Lina's grandmother's handwriting, traced from a postcard. Mormor never visited the bakery. She passed away in November 2024, two months before we signed the lease. The sign is for her.

We're five people now: Lina (founder, baker, occasionally answers the phone), Daniel (head baker, who came from Yumchaa in Stoke and runs the kitchen with cheerful tyranny), Priya (operations, who somehow gets every tin to every postcode in the West Midlands by 6pm), Joe (couriers and our customer-WhatsApp), and Emelie (Lina's sister, on a year off her Stockholm law firm, doing our accounts and writing most of the emails you receive from us).

What we believe.

Three things, mostly, and in this order.

One: rest is part of the work. Not after the work, in lieu of the work, or as a treat for finishing the work. Part of it. The Swedes have known this for a hundred and fifty years and the productivity numbers back them up. Most of British work culture is structured to make you feel slightly guilty about lunch. We think this is incorrect, and our tins are designed to make stopping for twenty minutes feel like a positive act, not a shameful one.

Two: ingredients are most of the answer. A £30 cookie should be a £30 cookie. We use Belgian Callebaut chocolate, Valrhona dark, Shipton Mill flour from a family-run mill in the Cotswolds, free-range eggs from a single farm in Worcestershire, Sicilian pistachio cream we import directly. We don't have a flavour scientist, we don't use any extracts or stabilisers beyond vanilla, and we don't claim secret techniques. It's mostly just good ingredients and being a bit obsessive about brown-butter temperatures.

Three: the tin matters. A cookie in a paper bag is a snack. A cookie in a powder-coated steel tin with a hand-painted Dala horse on the lid is a moment. The tin is what makes a £30 cookie feel like a £30 cookie, and it's also what people keep — for tea bags, sewing kits, Lego, in one customer's case her late father's wedding ring. We think about the tin almost as much as we think about the cookie.

What's next.

For 2026 we're working on three things: a partner kitchen in north Birmingham doing a proper gluten-free range, a smaller "fika-for-one" tin (two cookies, half the size, for solo desk fika), and a permanent collection point in central Birmingham so people can pop in for a tin without ordering ahead. We'll send you an email if any of these become real. Until then — thanks for reading, and we hope your next fika is a good one.

A short timeline.

2024 · April

The first tin

Twelve cookies, a £4 tin from John Lewis, one bicycle. A leaving-do for a digital agency in Edgbaston.

2024 · September

Mjölk launches

Brand identity built around the Swedish word for milk, and the cultural ritual of fika. First properly-branded tins go out.

2024 · November

Mormor passes

Lina's grandmother, whose recipe is the basis of The Original. We spent the week we returned baking through tears.

2025 · March

Floodgate Studios

Move into our Digbeth kitchen. First three industrial ovens. Daniel joins as head baker.

2025 · June

Same-day in three hours

We hit our operational goal: every Birmingham order delivered warm, in under three hours, every working day.

Dubai Chocolate launches

Our take on the cult favourite. Sells out in 47 minutes on launch day. Becomes a permanent fixture by November.
